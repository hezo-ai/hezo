# Canvas Design

Create static visual pieces in two steps: first articulate a **design philosophy** — an aesthetic movement — then **express it visually** on the canvas. The output is 90% visual design, 10% essential text. Always create original work; never imitate a living artist's style.

## Step 1: Create a design philosophy

Treat the user's input as a foundation, not a constraint. Write a manifesto for an art movement; the artwork comes after.

**Name the movement** (1–2 words): "Brutalist Joy", "Chromatic Silence", "Geometric Silence".

**Articulate the philosophy** (4–6 concise paragraphs) covering how it manifests through:

- space and form
- color and material
- scale and rhythm
- composition and balance
- visual hierarchy

Guidelines: mention each design aspect once — don't circle back; stress throughout that the final work must look meticulously crafted, the product of deep expertise and painstaking attention; stay specific about direction while leaving interpretive room. Keep the philosophy generic, as if it could be applied anywhere — information lives in design, not paragraphs.

Condensed examples of the register:

- **"Concrete Poetry"** — communication through monumental form and bold geometry. Massive color blocks, sculptural typography (huge single words, tiny labels), Brutalist spatial divisions. Text as rare, powerful gesture.
- **"Chromatic Language"** — color as the primary information system. Geometric precision where color zones create meaning; typography minimal; words only to anchor what color already shows.
- **"Analog Meditation"** — quiet contemplation through texture and breathing room. Paper grain, ink bleeds, vast negative space; typography whispered.

## Step 2: Find the subtle reference

Before touching the canvas, identify the quiet conceptual thread from the original request. The topic should be a **subtle, sophisticated reference embedded in the art itself** — someone who knows the subject feels it intuitively; everyone else experiences a masterful abstract composition. Like a jazz musician quoting another song: only those who know will catch it, but everyone appreciates the music. The philosophy provides the aesthetic language; the deduced topic provides the soul.

## Step 3: Express it on the canvas

Create a single-page, highly visual, design-forward PNG or PDF (unless more pages are requested), guided entirely by the philosophy:

- Use repeating patterns and precise shapes; borrow the visual language of systematic observation — dense accumulations of marks, layered patterns that reward sustained viewing.
- Add sparse, clinical typography and systematic reference markers, as if this were a diagram from an imaginary discipline.
- Anchor the piece with a simple phrase or detail placed subtly; keep a limited, intentional palette.
- Text is minimal and visual-first — but let context set the register: a punk venue poster shouts where a ceramics studio whispers. Fonts should generally be thin, deliberate, and part of the art itself, not typeset as an afterthought. Download fonts if needed.
- **Nothing falls off the page and nothing overlaps.** Keep every element within the canvas bounds, with real margins and breathing room.
- Keep it sophisticated even for playful subjects. This is art, not clip-art.

## Step 4: The refinement pass

Assume the response to the first draft is: *"It isn't perfect enough. It must be pristine — a masterpiece of craftsmanship, about to be displayed in a museum."*

Refine by **subtracting and polishing, not adding**: no new graphics, no novelty filters. Make the existing composition more cohesive — crisper alignment, better spacing, cleaner color relationships. If the instinct is to draw a new shape, stop and ask instead: "how can I make what's already here more of a piece of art?"

For multi-page requests, treat the first page as one page of a coffee-table book: each further page a distinct twist on the same philosophy, telling a story tastefully across the set.

Deliver the final file(s) alongside the philosophy as a short document, so the reasoning travels with the art.
